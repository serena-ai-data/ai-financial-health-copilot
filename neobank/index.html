01 · User problem

Users have many financial products, but no shared decision layer.

A user may have bank accounts, cards, insurance, investments and wallets across different providers. Each product can work perfectly in isolation while the user still struggles to answer three cross-product questions: What is my overall position? What matters most? What should I do next?

1

Fragmented money view

Balances, policies, debt and investments live in different products with different data structures.

2

Complex interpretation

Users must translate ratios, product terms and risk information before they can compare priorities.

3

Weak action priority

Information is abundant, but the next useful action is often unclear or buried behind product-specific prompts.

“I do not need another financial dashboard. I need to know what deserves attention first.”

Primary user

Working professionals using multiple financial products who are comfortable with digital finance but are not financial specialists. They value speed, clarity, visible reasoning and control.

02 · Product direction

Start with understanding. Expand only after the core value is proven.

Instead of launching every financial feature at once, the product starts with a reusable decision layer that can sit across banking, cards, insurance and investments.

Onboarding & consent→Add / connect context→Unified money view→Financial health→Priority action→AI explanation→Human-confirmed action

The MVP wedge already exists.

The live Financial Health Copilot demonstrates the middle of this journey today: structured inputs, review and correction, deterministic analysis, explainability, prioritised next action and a bounded AI Copilot.

04 · Roadmap

Broaden capability only as trust, data and controls mature.

The release sequence deliberately separates understanding, connected context, personalised actions and execution.

MVP

Understand

Unified financial context, financial-health analysis, explainability and priority action.

V2

Connect

Bank / wallet / insurance connectors, source traceability and recurring refresh.

V3

Personalise

Goal planning, contextual nudges, action ranking and experimentation.

V4

Execute

Human-confirmed transactions or product hand-offs with suitability and audit controls.

05 · Product trade-offs

Speed matters, but so do user control and financial truth.

The key product decisions are not simply feature choices. They determine how quickly the product can learn without creating avoidable trust, compliance or operational risk.

Breadth vs focus

Start with one high-value money-management problem rather than trying to ship a full financial super-app on day one.

Connected data vs manual MVP

Validate whether the insight is useful before investing in expensive integrations and exception handling.

AI autonomy vs user control

AI can explain and prioritise, but verified financial state and consequential execution remain controlled.

Speed vs compliance complexity

Move quickly on low-risk learning while reserving stronger controls for payments, suitability and high-impact actions.

07 · AI & trust boundaries

AI explains. Rules calculate. Users stay in control.

For financial products, the question is not only where AI can add value. It is also where AI must stop.

Deterministic source of truth

Verified balances, calculations, score logic and transaction state should remain reproducible and auditable.

Bounded AI interpretation

AI may summarise, explain, surface uncertainty and answer grounded questions about verified product state.

Human-confirmed execution

Consequential financial actions should not be silently executed by an explanation layer.

08 · Localisation hypothesis

Same platform logic. Different local financial problem.

A reusable product layer should still be prioritised around the realities of each market rather than translated feature-for-feature.

China

Potential emphasis on cross-provider fragmentation, cross-insurer organisation, liquidity, accessibility, fraud/scam awareness, sensitive-data consent and explainable guidance.

Southeast Asia

Depending on market: wallet-first journeys, remittance, accessible protection, financial inclusion, multicurrency needs and local product constraints.

Shared principle

Reuse the platform where possible, but let the local user problem, regulation, infrastructure and trust model drive prioritisation.
